Research 101

Curious about what research is, how it works, and what it entails? This page is your starting point for understanding the basics of research.

Definition

What is Research?

Research is a process of asking and testing questions about the world around us. It encompasses many different types of projects, purposes, and methods — and can be conducted in any field, from the sciences to the arts.

Purpose

Why Does It Matter?

Research helps answer questions that impact people's lives. The answers researchers find shape policies, products, treatments, and public programs. Community voice is central — when people share their needs, research becomes more relevant to the people it aims to serve.

Learn more →
Process

What are the Basic Steps?

  1. Identify a question What problem or gap in knowledge needs to be addressed?
  2. Review existing research What has already been studied and what has been found?
  3. Form a hypothesis What do you expect to find, or what do you want to learn?
  4. Design the study Decide how you'll collect information to test your hypothesis.
  5. Collect data Gather information through experiments, surveys, interviews, or other methods.
  6. Analyze and share Interpret the data and communicate your findings with others.

Research Disciplines

Are there different kinds of research?

There are two main types of research, which aim to answer different kinds of questions.

  1. Quantitative Research Works with numbers, patterns, and statistical analysis. Typically involes large datasets, groups of people, or sample sizes. Aims to find results that can apply broadly.
  2. Qualitative Research Works with words, stories, and experiences. Typically involves fewer people or smaller sample sizes. Aims to identify why something happens or what it means to the people experiencing it or impacted by it.

Data

What is data?

Data is the information researchers collect to answer their questions. It can be numbers, words, images, or other types of information. Researchers analyze data to find patterns, relationships, and insights that help them draw conclusions.

Methods

What Are Research Methods?

Research methods are the systematic approaches used to collect and analyze data. They help researchers ensure their findings are reliable and valid. A research team may use a variety of data collection methods, such as surveys, interviews, experiments, or observations, depending on the research question and goals.

Ethics

What are Research Ethics?

Research ethics are the principles and guidelines that govern how research is conducted. They ensure that studies are carried out in a way that respects the rights and welfare of participants.

Funding

How is Research Funded?

Research funding comes from various sources, including government agencies, private foundations, universities, and industry partners. Funding supports the costs of conducting research, such as equipment, materials, personnel, and participant compensation. Grant funding is often very competitive, and researchers must submit proposals that outline their research questions, methods, and expected outcomes to secure funding.

Purpose

How Are Results Communicated?

Researchers communicate their findings in various ways, including academic journals, conferences, reports, and public presentations. Sharing results allows other researchers to build on the work, informs policy and practice, and contributes to the broader knowledge base in the field. A poster presentation is a common way for researchers to share their work visually, while a research paper provides a detailed written account of the study and its findings. An abstract is a brief summary of the research, often used to introduce the study at conferences or in publications.
